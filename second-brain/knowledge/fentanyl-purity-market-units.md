# Illicit Fentanyl Purity & Market Units

Durable version of the **Illicit Fentanyl Purity & Market Units Database** artifact (compiled Jun
2026). Quantitative purity only (% by mass or mg/unit); detection/prevalence data excluded.
Purpose: clinical MME estimation research. → pairs with [MME estimation](mme-estimation.md).

## Headline numbers

- Programs with quantitative purity data: **10** (US + Canada).
- US cities with consumer-level data: **2** (Los Angeles, Philadelphia).
- **LA median purity 5.8%** (range 0.1–64.9%, n=353).
- DEA average pill content 2024: **1.94 mg/pill** (seizure-level, national).

## DEA Fentanyl Profiling Program (seizure-level, national)

Powder mean % by mass: CY2021 14.4% · CY2022 **19.2%** (peak) · CY2023 19.7% · CY2024 11.36%
(sharp decline begins) · FY2025 10.3%.

Pressed pills (mg/tablet): CY2019 1.7mg (27% ≥2mg) · CY2021 2.2mg (~44%) · CY2022 2.4mg (65%) ·
CY2023 2.3mg (67–76%) · CY2024 1.94mg (~50%) · FY2025 (29% ≥2mg).

⚠️ **Seizure ≠ consumer.** DEA FPP powder means (14–20%) run **4–5× higher** than consumer-facing
medians (3–6%) due to supply-chain dilution. Kilmer et al. (Addiction 2022) found consumer-level
median 3.5–4.0% in 2021. **Use community drug-checking data for clinical MME, not DEA seizure data.**

## Canada (comparator)

BC (BCCSU, n=8,122, 2021–2023) median **10.6%**; province rose 3.6% (2019) → peak 18.7% (Oct 2024)
→ ~9–12% (2026) as **fluorofentanyl** displaces fentanyl. Toronto much lower: median **1.1–3.7%**
(fluorofentanyl/methylfentanyl rising). Bardwell et al. (Victoria 2022): within-batch RSD ~25% vs
5% analytical error — "hot spots" are real.

## Market units (peer-reviewed)

**Philadelphia** is the only city with a peer-reviewed weight-quantified unit: bag (glassine) =
**0.03 g**; 10–16 bags/bundle; $5–10/bag (Montero, Bourgois & Friedman, JIED 2022/23, PMC10065983).
No peer-reviewed unit weights found for other US cities.

## Methodological caveats for MME
Consumer vs seizure level · formulation matters (powder 6.9% vs tar 1.4% vs pills 0.7% in LA) ·
sold-as matters (5× difference) · purity is not static (display the data date) · within-batch
variability is large · postmortem fentanyl blood levels **cannot** be back-calculated to
antemortem dose (3–5.5× redistribution within 24h; Gill et al. 2012).

**Not for clinical decision-making without regional validation.**
